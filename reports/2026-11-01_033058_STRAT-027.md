## 1. Executive summary

STRAT-027 tested whether a systematic, monthly-rebalanced, multi-stock portfolio built from
a single measurable factor beats buy-and-hold SPY and 1,000 random portfolios of the same
size and rebalancing, after risk and costs.

**Outcome: the study ran to completion and the answer is NO.** One factor (VALUE — trailing
earnings yield) passed the frozen development bar in 2021–2024, and then **failed** the
frozen one-shot holdout (Jan 2025 → Sep 2026): it beat SPY on return (20.2% vs 17.9%) and
Sharpe (1.65 vs 1.47), but landed only at the **73rd percentile** of the 1,000 random
portfolios (z = 0.58, paired t = 0.68) instead of the required 99th percentile / z ≥ 2.0.
Momentum, low volatility and quality failed in both windows.

The most valuable finding is methodological: in the holdout window the 95th percentile of
*random* 30-stock portfolios (27.6%/yr) was far above SPY (17.9%/yr), so "beats SPY" is
not evidence of an edge. The random-portfolio null is the test that discriminated signal
from luck, and it says there is no signal here.

**No production code, configuration, database, broker path, BUY threshold, C4 sleeve or
RSI(2) shadow was touched.**

## 6. Known risks and limitations

1. **Survivorship bias is reduced, not eliminated.** The source dataset includes 590
   Alpaca-inactive names, but Alpaca exposes no listing/delisting dates, so the universe is
   "broad historical", not point-in-time index membership.
2. **The holdout is 20 monthly periods in one strong bull market.** Low power and one regime.
3. **Multiple comparisons.** Four factors × two windows × a sensitivity grid; some
   development-window "wins" are luck. The one-shot holdout and the random-portfolio null
   are the controls against this.
4. **Concentration.** The development value result depended on ~10 of 150 names (~50% of
   the contribution).
5. **Annual-only fundamentals.** Quality/value are step functions that change about once a
   year; no quarterly XBRL is available locally.
6. **Flat cost model.** 10 bps per side; no spread/impact/market-depth model, no borrow
   (long only), no taxes, fractional shares assumed.
7. **Delisted names** are valued at their last available close; recovery beyond that is not
   modelled.
8. **The universe is broad**, not "S&P 500 members": it includes liquid small/mid caps
   screened only by ≥1M shares/day and a $5 price floor.
9. **STRAT-021 overlap.** Momentum, low volatility and quality/gross-profitability were
   already tested (STRAT-021, annual rebalancing, quintile sleeves, all FAIL vs SPY).
   STRAT-027 re-tests the same families with monthly rebalancing, a concentrated capped
   basket, a random-portfolio null and a real holdout. This is stated in the report; it is
   **not** a new factor family.
10. **One self-inflicted bug was found and fixed mid-study** (pivoted fundamentals rows
    mixed filings, so the as-of selector used ancient fiscal years). It inflated the
    development value result; the panel was rebuilt at fact level and every number was
    regenerated. Recorded here and in `DATA_AUDIT.md` so the next agent does not repeat it.
